Workshop Scope

Topic and Scope

Why behavior-only evaluations are no longer sufficient for model safety assurance, and how reverse-engineering circuits holds the key to robust alignment.

Recent advances in foundation models have enabled remarkable capabilities, but ensuring their behavior remains reliable and aligned with human intentions is a critical challenge. Behavioral evaluations alone are insufficient for strong alignment assurance, as safety fine-tuning can fail under adversarial pressure, and model outputs may not represent internal reasoning faithful to chain-of-thought.

The Workshop on Mechanistic Interpretability for Robust AI Alignment (MIRAI) focuses on reverse-engineering neural networks to understand the circuits, representations, and computations underlying model behavior. We bring together researchers to address a concrete question: what kinds of mechanistic evidence can improve our ability to trust, monitor, and repair advanced AI systems under distribution shift, adversarial pressure, and deployment?

Key Focus Areas

MIRAI emphasizes safety-relevant circuits, latent goals and plans, anomaly detection, model repair, refusal mechanisms, deception and alignment-faking detection, benchmarking of faithfulness, and scalable interpretability methods that keep pace with frontier models.

Submissions

Call for Papers

We invite original research contributions, position papers, benchmark and dataset papers, negative results, and work-in-progress submissions on topics related to mechanistic interpretability, AI alignment, transparency, and trustworthy AI. All submissions are non-archival (accepted papers will not appear in formal proceedings, allowing for future submission to other venues). Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Mechanistic interpretability of LLMs, VLMs, and multimodal foundation models
  • Circuit discovery, feature attribution, and representation analysis
  • Sparse autoencoders, dictionary learning, and feature-based interpretability
  • Understanding reasoning, planning, and agentic behavior in neural networks
  • Mechanistic approaches to alignment & safety
  • Detection and analysis of deceptive behavior, reward hacking, and specification gaming
  • Internal goal representations and objective identification in foundation models
  • Interpretability-driven interventions, model editing, and steering methods
  • Mechanistic evaluation of robustness, generalization, and out-of-distribution behavior
  • Causal analysis and mechanistic explanations of neural computations
  • Automated & scalable interpretability methods
  • Interpretability for reinforcement learning and autonomous agents
  • Alignment verification and auditing through mechanistic methods
  • Mechanistic transparency for reasoning and chain-of-thought analysis
  • Benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation frameworks for interpretability and alignment
  • Human-AI collaboration for model understanding and safety assessment
  • Interpretability-informed governance, auditing, and AI accountability
  • Theoretical foundations of mechanistic understanding and alignment
  • Limitations, risks, and open challenges in mechanistic interpretability

Speakers

Invited Speakers

Distinguished experts sharing foundational and applied work in mechanistic interpretability and AI safety.

Verena Rieser

Google DeepMind
Confirmed

Senior Staff Research Scientist at Google DeepMind, where she founded the VOICES team (Voices-of-all in alignment). Her team is a core contributor to Gemini with a mission to enhance model safety and usability for diverse communities. She has pioneered work in data-driven multimodal Dialogue Systems and Natural Language Generation, encompassing conversational RL agents and evaluation methodologies. She previously was a full professor at Heriot-Watt University, Edinburgh, and held a Royal Society Leverhulme Senior Research Fellowship.

Arthur Conmy

Google DeepMind
Confirmed

Senior Research Engineer at Google DeepMind on the Language Model Interpretability team, focusing on mechanistic interpretability and practical safety techniques for frontier models. He produced foundational work in automated circuit discovery, including Interpretability in the Wild (ICLR) and the ACDC algorithm (NeurIPS), and has pioneered activation probes in live deployments to detect misuse. He was previously a researcher at Redwood Research.

Sarah Wiegreffe

University of Maryland
Confirmed

Assistant professor in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Maryland, working on the explainability and interpretability of language models to make them more reliable, safe, and transparent. She has been honored as a 3-time Rising Star in EECS, Machine Learning, and Generative AI. She was previously a postdoc at the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington. She received her Ph.D. and M.S. degrees from Georgia Tech.

Flora Salim

UNSW Sydney
Confirmed

Professor in the School of Computer Science and Engineering at UNSW Sydney and the Deputy Director (Engagement) of the UNSW AI Institute. She is a leading expert in trustworthy and robust machine learning, serving as a Chief Investigator for the ARC Centre of Excellence for Automated Decision-Making and Society (ADM+S). Her research focuses on the robustness of Large Language Models (LLMs), vulnerabilities in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and aligning models through cognitive frameworks like Theory of Mind (ToM).

Tom Gedeon

Curtin University
Confirmed

Professor at the School of Electrical Engineering, Computing and Mathematical Sciences at Curtin University and Director of the Optus Centre for Artificial Intelligence. His research spans artificial intelligence, human-centered AI, affective computing, multimodal learning, computer vision, and human–AI interaction, with a strong emphasis on responsive and responsible AI systems. He has authored hundreds of publications and supervised numerous researchers across machine learning, computer vision, biomedical AI, and intelligent systems.
